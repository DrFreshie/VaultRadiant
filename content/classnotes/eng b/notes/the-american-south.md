# The American South

This unit examines how the American South is represented through journalism, podcast storytelling, fiction, and television. Rather than treating the region as a single, stable identity, students compare portrayals of place and people and consider how stereotypes, narrative perspective, dialect, and historical context shape those portrayals.

Hunter S. Thompson's gonzo journalism and Brian Reed's *S-Town* introduce questions of subjectivity, narrative non-fiction, and the relationship between storyteller and subject. Work on the rhetorical situation develops students' ability to discuss exigence, audience, and constraints. Mark Twain's *The Adventures of Huckleberry Finn* introduces Southern American English, irony, and the contrast between a narrator's understanding and that of the reader.

Extended reading of Carys Davies's *West* broadens the geographical focus to American expansion and the frontier. Students examine freedom, ambition, gender, and displacement, connecting individual choices to unequal opportunities. The opening episode of *Lovecraft Country* offers an audiovisual point of comparison for representations of America, race, and freedom.

## Key terms

*Representation, stereotype, region, narrative non-fiction, gonzo journalism, subjectivity, objectivity, rhetorical situation, exigence, audience, constraints, dialect, irony, characterisation, freedom, frontier, expansion, displacement.*

## Working methods and skills

- Close reading and comparison of representations across genres and media.
- Podcast listening with attention to narrative and audio techniques.
- Reading circles with rotating roles for summary, questions, vocabulary, connections, and illustration.
- Read-aloud practice and recorded imitation to develop pronunciation and awareness of dialect.
- Creative responses, including letters, diary entries, and alternative perspectives.
- Analytical paragraphs, funnel introductions, essay outlines, and work towards an analytical essay.
- Oral-exam-style presentations with a focus on characterisation.
- Grammar: nouns, subject–verb agreement, and active/passive voice.
