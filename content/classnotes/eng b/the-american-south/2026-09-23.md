## Warm-up: What does "west" mean?

Discuss:

- What ideas are connected to the word *west* in American culture?
- Is the West a place, a direction, a dream, or a myth?
- How might the South and the West be connected in stories about America?

## Introducing *West*

As we begin *West*, focus on movement, dreams, danger, and family.

Key questions for the text:

- Why do characters leave home?
- What do they hope to find?
- What does the journey cost them?
- How does the text challenge myths about America?

## Reading: opening section

Read the opening section of *West*.

Mark details about:

- place and landscape
- family relationships
- dreams or plans
- danger or uncertainty
- narrative perspective

## Setting and genre

### Discuss

1. What kind of world does the opening create?
2. Is the setting realistic, symbolic, hostile, hopeful, or strange?
3. What genre expectations do you have: adventure, tragedy, Western, Gothic, coming-of-age?

## Character notes

Create a character map.

For each central character, note:

- what they want
- what they fear
- what they know
- what they do not understand
- one quote or detail

## Exit-ticket

Write one prediction for the journey in *West*.
