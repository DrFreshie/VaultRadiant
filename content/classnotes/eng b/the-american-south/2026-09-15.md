## The Rhetorical Situation

- *Exigence* What is the (real) purpose of the text? What is the speaker/writer trying to achieve/change? A situation becomes rhetorical once you believe that you can change something in life by uttering something.
- *Audience* Who is the text meant for? What is the medium, and who is the specific audience of this medium? What is their social background, political and religious belief, age, nationality, gender, ethnicity, etc.? The will be the *mediators of change* who can act on the exigence (i.e. do something about the issue).
- *Constraints* The rhetorical situation will have som limitations and possibilities related to the context, the speaker/writer, and the mediators of change. These are the constraints. To make a change as likely as possible, what can then be said? What cannot?

## Gonzo analysis

### Discuss

1. What kind of narrator is Thompson?
2. Is he reliable? Why or why not?
3. What does he actually tell us about the Kentucky Derby?
4. What does he tell us about American society?
5. How are Southern people represented?
6. Does the essay criticise the South, America, rich people, crowds, journalism, or Thompson himself?
