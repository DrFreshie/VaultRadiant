# West V

---
[[classnotes/eng b/the-american-south/index|the american south]]

## ⋮⋮ Program

- *08.35* Warm up
- *08.45* Reading circle 
- *09.30* Break
- *09.45* Lovecraft Country
- *10.45* Post-watch work

### Post-watch work with *Lovecraft Country* episode 1

For only 20 minutes, keep it tightly focused on **connection-making**, not full analysis. The goal: use the episode as a lens for thinking about *West*, the American South, race, movement, danger, and genre.

#### 1. Quick emotional check-in — 2 minutes

Ask students to choose one word:

> “After watching, I feel / think: ___”

Give options on the board for ESL support:

**shocked, tense, confused, angry, curious, afraid, sad, interested, disturbed**

They can say it aloud or write it privately.

#### 2. Pair discussion: What kind of “America” is shown? — 5 minutes

In pairs, students answer:

1. Where did the characters feel unsafe?
2. Who had power in this episode?
3. What rules, spoken or unspoken, controlled Black people’s movement?

Useful sentence starters:

- “The characters were unsafe when…”
- “Power belonged to…”
- “One rule was…”
- “The episode shows America as…”

#### 3. Connect to *West* — 7 minutes

Put this comparison question on the board:

> In *West*, travel often seems connected to adventure, discovery, or escape. In *Lovecraft Country*, what does travel mean?

Make a simple T-chart:

| *West* | *Lovecraft Country* |
|---|---|
| travel westward | travel through Jim Crow America |
| wilderness/frontier | roads, towns, police, racism |
| danger from nature/unknown | danger from people/systems |
| dream/quest | survival/fear |

Then ask:

> How does the episode change our idea of “the American journey”?

Possible answer frame:

- “In *West*, the journey is about ___, but in *Lovecraft Country*, the journey is about ___.”
- “Both texts show that America is ___.”
- “The episode complicates the idea of freedom because ___.”

#### 4. Mini genre question — 4 minutes

Ask:

> Why mix historical racism with monsters?

Students can discuss briefly or write one sentence.

Guide them toward:

- The “real” horror is racism.
- The monsters make visible the fear and violence already present.
- Horror helps viewers feel danger, not just understand it intellectually.

Sentence starter:

> “The monsters are scary, but the episode suggests that ___ is also monstrous.”

#### 5. Exit ticket — 2 minutes

Have them write one of these:

1. “One connection between *West* and *Lovecraft Country* is…”
2. “One question I now have about the American South is…”
3. “The episode made me think differently about America because…”

Collect these to shape the next lesson.

#### Main takeaway for students

> Both *West* and *Lovecraft Country* are about journeys through America, but they ask us to think about who is allowed to travel freely, who is in danger, and what kinds of stories America tells about itself.
