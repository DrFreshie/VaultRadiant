## Warm-up: What do you know about the South?

1. Write five words you associate with the American South.
2. Where do those ideas come from: films, music, history, news, memes, school?
3. Which words are about place, and which words are about people?
4. What is the danger of beginning a unit with stereotypes?

## Unit introduction

In this unit, we examine the American South as both a real region and a cultural idea.

- How is the South represented by outsiders?
- How do Southern texts use decay, violence, humour, and tradition?
- What happens when place becomes part of identity?
- Who gets to define a place: insiders or outsiders?

## Southern stereotypes and Southern Gothic

### Pair discussion

- What makes a setting feel "Southern"?
- What does the word *gothic* suggest?
- Why might old houses, family secrets, religion, poverty, heat, and decay be useful in Southern stories?

### Notes

Southern Gothic often includes:

- decaying settings
- grotesque or extreme characters
- family secrets
- social hypocrisy
- racism and historical guilt
- humour mixed with horror
- outsiders looking in

## Hunter S. Thompson and gonzo journalism

Gonzo journalism is a style where the journalist becomes part of the story. The writer's voice, opinions, exaggerations, and experiences are not hidden.

### Questions

- What is the difference between objective journalism and personal journalism?
- Can a text be truthful even if it is exaggerated?
- What do we expect from a journalist covering a sports event?

## Reading

Begin reading *The Kentucky Derby Is Decadent and Depraved*.

While reading, mark passages that show:

- Southern place and atmosphere
- the narrator's attitude
- exaggeration or humour
- decadence or depravity
- descriptions of people as spectacle
