## Warm-up: Old houses and old stories

Look at the title *A Rose for Emily*.

Discuss:

- What could a rose symbolise?
- What expectations do you have when a story begins with a funeral?
- Why are old houses often important in Gothic stories?

## William Faulkner and Southern Gothic

William Faulkner often wrote about the American South, memory, old families, social change, and the burden of the past.

### Key ideas

- the past affecting the present
- old social hierarchies
- decay and isolation
- community gossip
- mystery and withheld information

## Reading: Part I-II

Read the first parts of *A Rose for Emily*.

While reading, mark:

- descriptions of Emily's house
- descriptions of Emily herself
- what the town thinks about her
- signs that the story is not told chronologically

## Timeline work

Faulkner does not tell the story in simple chronological order.

Create two timelines:

1. The order in which events are told.
2. The order in which events actually happen.

## Characterisation of Emily

Answer with evidence:

1. How does the town view Miss Emily?
2. How is her house connected to her character?
3. What does Emily represent from the past?
4. Is the narrator sympathetic, judgemental, curious, or cruel?

## Exit-ticket

Write one question the story has created for you so far.

## Materials

- *A Rose for Emily* by William Faulkner
