## Recap

> > [!task] In pairs:
> > 1. Make a list of the casts of this society, and the jobs reserved for the members of these casts.
> > 2. One student is an Alpha from the Hatchery, the other an Epsilon. The Alpha student outlines the scientific advances and explain the Bokanovsky process so an Epsilon can understand it, and the Epsilon asks questions for clarification.
>
> > [!task] Individually:
> > 1. Finish reading the first chapter of Brave New World
> > 2. Make a list of the casts of this society, and the jobs reserved for the members of these casts.
> > 3. Write an explanation of the Bokanovsky Process addressed to an Epsilon – simplify the scientific explanation so the Epsilon can understand you but stay true to the facts. Use informal English and be as objective as possible.

## World building

> [!pullquote]
> A squat grey building of only thirty-four stories. Over the main entrance the words, CENTRAL LONDON HATCHERY AND CONDITIONING CENTRE, and, in a shield, the World State’s motto, COMMUNITY, IDENTITY, STABILITY.

> [!no-slide]
> In science fiction, the setting is often a character in itself. The above quote is however the only description of the outside world we get in the first chapter. What we get instead is a description of how society is structured. Can we use this to imagine how such a society might look?

> [!task] Create
> 1. In the form of a poster (it may be digital), make a visual representation of the World State focusing on the various strata of society (Alphas, Betas, etc.) and their abilities and roles. A number of (the fictive) scientific facts must be included as well as the World State’s motto. Find illustrative pictures online, draw them, or take photos. 
> 2. Find five quotes that inspired your vision of the world.

## Adaptation

> [!no-slide]
> Watch the first 18 minutes of the tv-show Brave New World and do the reflection exercise below.

In the novel, the world state motto is "Community, Identity, Stability", in the show New London has three rules: "No privacy. No family. No monogamy".

> [!task] Reflect
> 1. Write for 5 minutes about the the show's interpretation of the brave new world of the novel. Did the world look like you had imagined it? What's the connection between the New London rules and the World State motto?

## Glossary

- **Cognitive estrangement:** Science fiction makes familiar things seem strange so we can look at our own world in a new way. Its unfamiliar world follows an understandable logic.
- **Info-dump:** A passage that gives the reader a large amount of background information about the fictional world at once. This can happen through a narrator’s explanation or a character’s speech, such as the Director’s tour of the Hatchery.
