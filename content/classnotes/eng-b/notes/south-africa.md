# South Africa

This unit explores South Africa under apartheid and the transition towards democracy. Students examine how racial classification, segregation, and unequal rights shaped everyday life, and how resistance to apartheid challenged the political system. Historical background material, legislation, and photographs provide context for the study of speeches, fiction, memoir, and film.

Speeches by Hendrik Verwoerd and Nelson Mandela offer contrasting perspectives on apartheid and the struggle against it. Students analyse how speakers present their arguments and address their audiences, connecting rhetorical choices to historical context. Nadine Gordimer's “Once upon a Time” and Don Mattera's “Die Bushie Is Dood” introduce fictional representations of fear, racial divisions, and life under apartheid. Trevor Noah's memoir and interview connect personal experience with the laws governing race and identity.

Clint Eastwood's *Invictus* extends the focus to Mandela's presidency and the challenges of reconciliation and nation-building after apartheid. Across the unit, students practise speech analysis and fiction analysis, relating individual texts to apartheid legislation, the ANC, and Mandela's role in South African history. Grammar focuses on pronouns.
