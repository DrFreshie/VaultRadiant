# The FLIRT model

## F — Form

- How is the poem structured into lines and stanzas? Does it follow a particular poetic form?
- Are there changes, contrasts or developments in its structure?
- How does the form support or contrast with the content?

## L — Language

- What kinds of words does the speaker use: formal, informal, emotional, concrete or abstract?
- What tone do the word choices and sentence structures create?
- Who is speaking, and who seems to be addressed? Do not automatically equate the speaker with the poet.

## I — Imagery and rhetorical devices

- Look for metaphors, similes, personification, symbols, allusion, antithesis, repetition, alliteration and onomatopoeia.
- What images or associations do they create?
- How do they support the poem's tone or ideas?

## R — Rhyme / rhythm

- Is there a rhyme scheme, or is the poem written in free verse?
- What patterns of stress, repetition or pauses can you hear when reading it aloud?
- Do sentences continue across line breaks (enjambment), or do they pause at the end of lines?
- How do these choices affect pace, emphasis and meaning?

## T — Theme

- What central ideas or conflicts does the poem explore?
- What does it suggest about these ideas? A topic might be *identity*; an interpretation of its theme explains what the poem says about identity.
- How do form, language, imagery and rhythm support your interpretation?
