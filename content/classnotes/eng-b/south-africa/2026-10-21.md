## English as Lingua Franca

**Lingua franca – have you heard this term before?**

A lingua franca is a language used by people to communicate when they do not share a common language. Throughout history there have been different lingua francas such as Russian, Spanish, French, German and Arabic, but today English is **the** lingua franca also referred to as ELF.

### English as A Lingua Franca (ELF)

- ELF is characterized by the fact that it is a *contact language* between people who do not share the same language.
- ELF is therefore the third language.
- Often, people using ELF do not share the same (national) culture.

One of the reasons why English has become the lingua franca of the world has to do with the fact that Britain was a great colonial power and therefore, the English language spread throughout the colonies as the language to be used in politics, for trade and in education. In 1922, the British Empire covered almost a quarter of the total land on Earth and about one-fifth of the world's population lived in the British Empire.

### Kachru's Model

Another way of illustrating the importance of the English language is by using Kachru's 1985 three-circle model of the expansion of English. Kachru divides the world of Englishes into three concentric circles.

<div style="text-align:center;">
  <img src="https://files.edaptio.com/uploads/147038/9a244b4a98b6398e5225c0856d1f8332/pasted-image-1792412828848.png" alt="Kachru's Model" style="max-width:637px; height:auto;" />
</div>

- **The inner circle:** English in the inner circle is represented by countries where English is the native language (ENL). English spoken in the inner circle is norm-providing. This means that English in the inner circle sets the standard of English. The countries in the inner circle are: the United Kingdom, the United States, Australia, New Zealand and Canada.
- **The outer circle:** English spoken in the outer circle is represented by countries where English functions as a second language (ESL). These countries are characterized by being former colonies from the period Kachru calls the second diaspora. English spoken in the outer circle is norm-developing. This means that Englishes in the outer circle have become institutionalized and have developed their own standards of English. The countries in the outer circle are countries such as: Nigeria, Jamaica, Sri Lanka and India.
- **The expanding circle:** English spoken in the expanding circle is represented by countries where English functions as a foreign language (EFL). These countries are characterized by not using English as an official language but using it to communicate. The English standard is set by the native speakers of the inner circle. English spoken in the expanding circle is based on performance varieties. Countries in the expanding circle are countries such as: Denmark, China, Japan, Israel, Germany.

## Englishes in South Africa

As in other African countries, several varieties of English are spoken in South Africa. However, South Africa differs in the way that some 4.7 million people of about 56 million speak English as their first language. Of these 4.7 million there are: 33% white, 24% black, 22% Indians (Indians arrived to SA from 1860, and by the 1960s and 70s, English had become their first language) and about 19% mixed race.

## Apartheid

From 1948 to 1994, South Africa was under apartheid, which was a system of legal racial segregation (apartheid = separateness in Afrikaans). Thus, whites and blacks/coloureds were separated in all ways possible, e.g. education, medical care and they even lived in segregated residential areas. Also beaches and park benches were reserved for whites and blacks/coloureds respectively.

This racist regime was maintained through fear, violence and strict control. It was finally overturned after many years of social and political unrest and marches led by Nelson Mandela and the ANC.
