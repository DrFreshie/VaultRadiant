## Identity

This introductory unit examines public and private identities and how family, possessions, role models, memories, and personal narratives shape our sense of self. Selected chapters of *What Remains of Edith Finch* provide a shared point of reference, while poetry, photography, podcast material, and television offer contrasting perspectives.

### Texts

- Giant Sparrow: *What Remains of Edith Finch* — selected chapters and ending through shared playthroughs.
- Paul Laurence Dunbar: *We Wear the Mask* — poem.
- Emily Esfahani Smith: *The Two Kinds of Stories We Tell Ourselves* — article.
- *This American Life*: *Golden Calf*, Act One, “Bowing Before the Famous” — podcast.
- Silvia de Couët: *The Fly Nobody Programmed* — article.
- *Severance* — television excerpt.

## Voice

This unit investigates voice as narrative perspective, style, self-expression, public speaking, and the opportunity to be heard. Students distinguish explicit information from implicit meaning and examine how language and perspective shape themes and messages. Reading and representation, social exclusion, and the relationship between personal confidence and public responsibility recur across the texts.

### Texts

- Stephen Fry: *Have You Heard of Oscar Wilde?* — non-fiction essay.
- Gregory Maguire: *How th'Irth Wint Rong by Hapless Joey@Homeskool.guv* — short story.
- Katherine Mansfield: *The Doll's House* — short story.
- Jacinda Ardern: *Address to the UN General Assembly* (2019) — speech.
- Tom Hooper: *The King's Speech* — feature film, including analysis of the king's wartime address as presented in the film.

## The Future Is Written

This unit uses science fiction to explore identity, autonomy, memory, grief, and technological progress. *Brave New World* introduces utopia, dystopia, and world-building. *The Relive Box*, *Tell Me an Ending*, and “Be Right Back” examine the attraction and consequences of recovering, removing, or recreating the past. Non-fiction on human–robot relationships supports ethical discussion.

Work on George W. Bush's space-policy speech and *Settled In* compares political and fictional visions of exploration, AI, and human futures beyond Earth. Students connect American values, Manifest Destiny, and the frontier to visions of progress. Activities include comparative discussion, scene analysis, rhetorical analysis, oral presentation, audience-specific writing, and cohesive analytical paragraphs. Visual storytelling is introduced in *On a Sunbeam*.

### Texts and materials

- Aldous Huxley: *Brave New World*, Chapter 1 — novel excerpt.
- *Brave New World*, Season 1, Episode 1 — first 18 minutes of the television adaptation.
- T. Coraghessan Boyle: *The Relive Box* — short story.
- Jo Harkin: *Tell Me an Ending* — novel excerpt.
- *Black Mirror*: “Be Right Back”, Season 2, Episode 1 — television.
- Louisa Hall: *How We Feel About Robots That Feel* — article.
- George W. Bush: *Remarks on US Space Policy*, 14 January 2004 — speech.
- Tim Dudenhoefer: *Settled In* — short story.
- *Can AI 'cure' grief?* and an online video featuring Elon Musk's vision of a spacefaring civilisation — supplementary videos.
