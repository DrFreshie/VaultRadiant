# Voice

This unit develops students' ability to identify a text's voice and to express their own ideas in English. Voice is explored as narrative perspective, style, self-expression, public speaking, and the opportunity to be heard. Students distinguish between explicit information and implicit meaning and practise explaining a fiction text's themes and a non-fiction text's message with evidence.

Stephen Fry's account of reading Oscar Wilde opens discussion of reading, identity, and representation. Gregory Maguire's unconventional narrator and Katherine Mansfield's *The Doll's House* develop attention to language, perspective, characterisation, and social exclusion. Jacinda Ardern's UN speech introduces the rhetorical situation, rhetorical devices, and modes of appeal. Comparison with Donald Trump's speaking style highlights how delivery and language choices address different audiences.

*The King's Speech* brings together the personal and political dimensions of voice through the relationship between speech, confidence, authority, and responsibility.
