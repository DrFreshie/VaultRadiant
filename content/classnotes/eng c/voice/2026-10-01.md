## Warm up

- What makes a voice convincing online?
- Think of a person or account you follow on social media. What kind of voice do they create through images, captions, comments and tone?
- Discuss: is this voice always authentic? Why / why not?

## Overview

What analytical tools have we worked with so far in this unit?

- *Voice*: Who is speaking, and what kind of voice is created?
- *Audience*: Who is the voice directed at?
- *Purpose / exigence*: What is the speaker or text trying to achieve?
- *Context*: What situation, society or conflict shapes the voice?
- *Characterisation*: What do actions, language and relationships reveal about a character?
- *Point of view*: Whose perspective do we see the world through?
- *Rhetorical devices*: e.g. repetition, contrast, metaphor, list of three and inclusive language
- *Modes of appeal*: ethos, pathos and logos
- *Message*: What does the text suggest or criticise?

Today we will use these tools to analyse *Nosedive* from *Black Mirror*.

## Nosedive

As you watch, choose three important scenes and write down:

1. What happens in the scene?
2. How does Lacie present herself?
3. How do other people respond to her voice or behaviour?
4. What does the scene suggest about social media, popularity or identity?

## Group work

In groups, discuss the following questions:

- Describe Lacie's public voice at the beginning of the episode. Which words would you use about it?
- When does her voice begin to change? Find specific examples.
- How does the rating system affect people's language, behaviour and relationships?
- Who has power in this society, and how is that power shown?
- What is the episode's message about authenticity and performance?
- How is *Nosedive* connected to the theme of voice?

## Exit ticket

Write 5-7 sentences answering this question:

> In *Nosedive*, is Lacie losing her voice or finding it? Explain your answer with at least one example from the episode.
