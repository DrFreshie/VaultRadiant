## Rights of the reader

- Which of the rights of the reader introduced by Daniel Pennac do you consider most important? Why?
- Are any of the rights unnecessary? Which and why?
- Farrah Serroukh from the Centre for Literacy in Primary Education proposes the 11th Right of the Reader: the right of every reader to be visible. How do you interpret this right, and how important do you think it is?
- If you were to add the 12th Right of the Reader, what would you suggest and why?
- Discuss these questions:
     - What do you read for pleasure? – and for information?
     - When do you read for pleasure? – and for information?
     - Where do you read for pleasure? – and for information?
     - Do you read for pleasure in school? – and for information?
     - What was the last text you read for pleasure?
     - Why do you (not) read for pleasure? – and for information?

## Vocabulary

- For each of the following adjectives find a synonym and an antonym: *shattering* · *terrible* · *liberating* · *stimulating* · *appalling* · *wonderful* · *incredible* 
- With what would you connect the following nouns? Suggest a superordinate term (overordnet begreb) for: *titillation* · *possibility* · *chance of encounter* · *vindication* 
- Suggest a superordinate term for these verbs: *bowed down before* · *worshipped* · *embraced* · *befriended* 
- An oxymoron is a technical term which consists of two contradictory elements like sweet sorrow. How would you rephrase these two expressions from the text into standard English? *dark blessing* · *awful privilege*
- Suggest a superordinate term for these nouns: *incredible glamour* · *possibility* · *power* · *excitement* · *pleasure* 

## Reading

- Read until line 55 and note what Stephen Fry has to say about libraries and about what attracted him to the works of Oscar Wilde.

## First impression

- What does he realize about the effect of reading that he has not experienced before?
- Which adjectives does Stephen Fry use about his experience of reading that Oscar Wilde was gay?

## Reading continued

- Note ten details (explicit information) from the text
- Discuss:
    - Gender or libraries – which is more important in the text? Find evidence in the text to prove your point.
    - What is the message of the text? Find evidence to support your view. Which of the now 12 Rights of the Reader best agree(s) with the message of the text?

## Explanation

- Imagine that one of your friends was absent from this class. Write them an email / a letter in which you sum up the main issues of the lesson (200-400 words).

## Free genre

- Think of a piece of music or a picture which reflects what you consider the most important aspects of this text. Write briefly in poetry or prose about the links between the text and the piece of art.
