1. Determine the type of poem
2. Use the FLIRT-model to analyse the poem

## Types of poems

- **Didactic poems:** clear message or moral; meant to teach or explain something directly.  
  *Examples: ["Do Not Stand at My Grave and Weep"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Do_Not_Stand_at_My_Grave_and_Weep), ["Eternity"](https://poets.org/poem/eternity), ["If—"](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Rewards_and_Fairies/If%E2%80%94)*
- **Dramatic monologue:** an imagined speaker addresses a silent listener; often a persona poem.  
  *Examples: ["Maximus, to himself"](https://www.poetryfoundation.org/poems/47494/maximus-to-himself), ["Lady Lazarus"](https://www.poetryfoundation.org/poems/49000/lady-lazarus)*
- **Elegy:** poem of mourning for someone who is dead; often moves from lament to praise to consolation.  
  *Examples: ["O Captain! My Captain!"](https://poets.org/anthology/whitman-0), ["The Haw Lantern"](https://www.poetryfoundation.org/poets/seamus-heaney)*
- **Epic poems:** long narrative poems with a heroic protagonist, legendary adventures, and historical or mythical importance.  
  *Examples:* *[Beowulf](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/16328), [The Cantos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Cantos), [Paradise Lost](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/20)*
- **Epigrams:** very short poems, often witty or satirical, focused on a single thought or event.  
  *Example: ["Candy is dandy, but liquor is quicker"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ogden_Nash#%22Candy_is_dandy,_but_liquor_is_quicker%22)*
- **Prose poems:** written in prose form but read like poetry; often use repetition, symbols, and metaphors.  
  *Example: ["Of Memory and Distance"](https://poets.org/poem/memory-and-distance)*
- **Limericks:** funny, light-hearted five-line poems with the rhyme scheme **aabba**.
- **Slam poetry:** performance-based poetry with live audience participation; developed by Marc Kelly Smith in 1980s Chicago.

## FLIRT-model

#### F — Form
- What structure does the poem have?
- Does the structure have a certain effect on the reader?
- Is there any correspondence between structure and content?

#### L — Language
- What language is used?
- Is the language emotive, persuasive, formal, informal etc.?
- Which sentence structures, verbs, adjectives, and adverbs are used, and with what effect?

#### I — Imagery and rhetorical devices
- Is there use of metaphors, similes, antithesis, onomatopoeia, allusion, repetition, or alliteration?
- What is the effect?

#### R — Rhyme/rhythm
- Does the poem rhyme?
- Which rhyme scheme is used, and what is the effect?

#### T — Theme
- Is there a message or theme in the poem?
- Where is it visible?
