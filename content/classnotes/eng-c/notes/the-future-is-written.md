# The Future Is Written

This unit approaches science fiction as a way of exploring present-day hopes, anxieties, and ethical dilemmas. Students consider how imagined technologies affect identity, autonomy, memory, grief, and human relationships, and compare utopian ambitions with dystopian consequences.

The opening chapter of *Brave New World* introduces world-building, social conditioning, and the distinction between utopia and dystopia. Work with its television adaptation develops comparison across media. *The Relive Box*, an excerpt from *Tell Me an Ending*, and “Be Right Back” examine the appeal and cost of recovering, removing, or recreating the past. Non-fiction on human–robot relationships supports discussion of emotional attachment and artificial intelligence.

The unit then broadens to visions of space exploration. George W. Bush's space-policy speech connects progress and international cooperation to American history, Manifest Destiny, and the frontier. Tim Dudenhoefer's *Settled In* allows students to compare political and fictional visions of journeys to Mars, including the role of AI. *On a Sunbeam* introduces visual storytelling in a webcomic.
