\textbf{VOCABULARY}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*., leftmargin=*]
  \item Apocalypse (Greek origin) means revelation and is used about Judgment Day in a biblical sense and the end of the world as described in the Book of Revelations, the last book of the New Testament in the Bible. The prefix post- means `after'. In post-apocalyptic literature, the setting is typically one in which the end of the world has already taken place and the characters are trying to survive and get on in a new world. The event that caused the world to collapse may be disease, war, or manmade and natural disasters.
  \begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{\alph*.}, leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item Find and explain the odd one out. Pay attention to prefixes and roots respectively: \textit{posthumous $\cdot$ postnatal $\cdot$ posture $\cdot$ postgraduate}
    \item Find and explain the odd one out. Pay attention to prefixes and roots respectively: \textit{post-war $\cdot$ post office $\cdot$ postmodernism $\cdot$ post-mortem}
    \item Find and explain the odd one out. Pay attention to prefixes and roots respectively: \textit{postponement $\cdot$ post-it-note $\cdot$ post-industrial $\cdot$ postscript}
  \end{enumerate}

  \item Topos (Greek origin) means place. The prefix u- means `no-' and the prefix dys- means `lacking' or `bad'. Utopias typically describe a society which is `perfect' from the point of view of the narrator whereas dystopias describe the opposite. Utopias and dystopias are frequently found in science fiction.
