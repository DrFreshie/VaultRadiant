\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=-0em]
\item A fight breaks out between servants of the Capulet and Montague houses, and as a result, Prince Escalus proclaims that fighting is prohibited, and that anyone who disturbs the peace will be sentenced to death. Exeunt all.
\item Elsewhere, Benvolio vows to help Romeo forget his unrequited love for Rosaline. Exeunt all.
\item Lord Capulet talks with Count Paris about Paris's plans to marry Juliet, and invites him to a feast to begin wooing her. Exeunt all.
\item Romeo and Benvolio plan to attend this same party, even though they are Montagues, sworn enemies of the Capulets. Exeunt all.
\item Before the feast, Lady Capulet asks Juliet to consider Paris as a potential husband. Juliet says that she’ll observe him at the feast to see what she thinks of him. Exeunt all.
\item Mercutio, Romeo, and Benvolio meet outside the Capulet home, wearing masks. Mercutio mocks Romeo for being so heartbroken over Rosaline. Exeunt all but Romeo.
\item Romeo says that he has a bad feeling that the night will set fate in motion and lead to his untimely death, but decides to go in anyway.
\item At the feast, Romeo and Juliet see each other for the first time and immediately fall in love. Tybalt wants to fight Romeo for sneaking in, but Capulet forbids him to. Exeunt all.
\item After the feast, Romeo climbs over a wall onto the Capulet property, planning to find Juliet. He sees Juliet at a balcony above him, and hears her say that she wishes he would abandon his last name, or that she could forsake her own, so that they could be together.
\item Romeo surprises Juliet by calling out to her from below, and the two profess their love for each other. Juliet promises to send a messenger the following day to make sure Romeo is serious about marriage. Exeunt all.
The \item next morning, Romeo asks Friar Lawrence to wed himself and Juliet. Friar Lawrence agrees despite his concern that the relationship is moving too quickly, and he hopes the marriage will end the feud between the Capulets and Montagues. Exeunt all.
\item Benvolio tells Mercutio that Tybalt sent a letter to Romeo’s house. Mercutio guesses that Tybalt is challenging Romeo to a duel, and tells Benvolio that Tybalt is a strong sword-fighter. Exeunt all.
\item Juliet’s Nurse talks to Romeo and agrees to send Juliet to Friar Lawrence later that day, so that they can wed.
She returns to Juliet and relays the good news, and Juliet leaves to meet Romeo and the friar. Exeunt all.
\item Friar Lawrence warns Romeo that marrying Juliet might have dangerous consequences, but when Juliet arrives, he relents and agrees to perform the marriage ceremony. Exeunt all.
\item While walking around the city, Mercutio and Benvolio run into Tybalt and a group of Capulet servants, and Mercutio and Tybalt start to argue.
\item Romeo walks up and Tybalt immediately challenges him to a fight, but Romeo refuses. Mercutio, infuriated, fights Tybalt instead.
\item When Romeo steps in to break up the fight, Tybalt reaches under Romeo’s arm and fatally stabs Mercutio. Romeo, enraged and heartbroken over the death of his best friend, kills Tybalt, then flees the scene. Exeunt Romeo.
\item Prince Escalus arrives and demands that Romeo be exiled, saying that if he does not leave the city, he’ll be executed for disrupting the peace. Exeunt all.
\item The Nurse tells Juliet that Romeo has killed Tybalt and has been exiled, but says that she will make sure Romeo comes to Juliet for their wedding night. Exeunt Juliet.
\item The Nurse finds Romeo and gives him a ring from Juliet. Friar Lawrence scolds Romeo harshly for killing Tybalt, then advises him to spend the night with Juliet and then flee to Mantua, with the hope that Prince Escalus might eventually change his mind about Romeo’s exile. Exeunt all.
\item Meanwhile, Lord Capulet promise Count Paris that Juliet will marry him in three days. Exeunt Paris.
\item Lord Capulet tells Juliet the news, but Juliet says that she won’t go through with the marriage, and her father angrily claims that if she doesn’t, he’ll disown her and throw her out of the house. Exeunt all.
\item The next morning, Romeo leaves Juliet after their wedding night, and Juliet heads to Friar Lawrence to see if he can help prevent her marriage to Paris. Friar Lawrence gives Juliet a vial of sleep-inducing potion and tells her that once she drinks it, she’ll enter into a deathlike state for 48 hours. Her parents and Paris will believe that she’s dead, and will place her in the Capulet tomb. In the meantime, Friar Lawrence will send word to Romeo of the plan, and when Juliet awakes, Romeo will be there to take her to Mantua. Exeunt Friar Lawrence.
\item Juliet returns home and tells her father that she agrees to marry Paris. Once alone, she worries about what could go wrong, and has a vision of Tybalt’s ghost searching for Romeo, but she puts her fears aside and drinks the potion.
The next day, her parents begin to prepare for Juliet’s wedding to Paris. Then, they discover her seemingly lifeless body and mourn until Friar Lawrence arrives. He tells them to prepare to bring her body to the Capulet tomb. Exeunt all.
\item In Mantua, Balthasar tells Romeo that Juliet is dead. Romeo is devastated, and buys poison from an apothecary. He plans to return to Verona, commit suicide in Juliet’s tomb, and lay beside her in her grave that night. Exeunt all.
\item Back in Verona, Friar Lawrence sees Friar John, who was supposed to deliver a letter to Romeo explaining that Juliet had faked her death and Romeo should meet her in the Capulet tomb. Friar Lawrence is shocked to find out that Friar John’s journey was delayed, and he never gave Romeo the letter.
\item Friar Lawrence makes a plan to rescue Juliet once she wakes up, and hide her until Romeo arrives. Friar Lawrence writes another letter to Romeo explaining the situation, and sends Friar John off to deliver it. He doesn't know that Romeo has already heard the news of Juliet’s death, and that he has made plans to die beside her. Exeunt all.
\item Paris sees Romeo mourning outside Juliet’s tomb, and the two fight.
\item Romeo kills Paris, then sees Juliet’s “corpse”, drinks the apothecary’s poison, and dies. 
\item Juliet wakes, finds Romeo dead, and stabs herself.
\item Friar Lawrence explains what happened, and the Capulets and Montagues agree to end their feud.
\end{itemize}
