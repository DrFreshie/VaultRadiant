\StageDir{Enter MERCUTIO, BENVOLIO, Page, and Servants}

\begin{drama*}

\benspeaks I pray thee, good Mercutio, let's retire:\\
The day is hot, the Capulets abroad,\\
And, if we meet, we shall not 'scape a brawl;\\
For now, these hot days, is the mad blood stirring.

\mercspeaks Thou art like one of those fellows that when he enters the confines of a tavern, \glos{claps me}{banker}{vb.} his sword upon the table and says 'God send me no need of thee!' and by the operation of the second cup draws it on the \glos{drawer}{bartender}{sb.}, when indeed there is no need.

\benspeaks Am I like such a fellow?

\mercspeaks Come, come, thou art as hot a Jack in thy mood as any in Italy, and as soon moved to be moody, and as soon moody to be moved.

[...]

\benspeaks By my head, here come the Capulets.

\mercspeaks By my heel, I care not.

\StageDir{Enter TYBALT and others}

\tybspeaks Follow me close, for I will speak to them. Gentlemen, good e'en'; a word with one of you.

\mercspeaks And but one word with one of us? Couple it with something; make it a word and a blow.

\tybspeaks You shall find me apt enough to that, sir, an you will give me occasion.

\mercspeaks Could you not take some occasion without giving?

\tybspeaks Mercutio, thou \glos{consort'st}{er ven med / spiller sammen med}{vb.} with Romeo,--

\mercspeaks Consort! What, dost thou make us \glos{minstrels}{spillemænd}{sb.}? An thou make minstrels of us, look to hear nothing but discords. Here's my fiddlestick; here's that shall make you dance. \glos{'Zounds}{ved kristi sår, bandeord}{udr.}, consort!

\benspeaks We talk here in the public haunt of men.\\
Either withdraw unto some private place,\\
And reason coldly of your grievances,\\
Or else depart. Here all eyes gaze on us.

\mercspeaks Men's eyes were made to look and let them gaze.\\
I will not budge for no man's pleasure, I.

\StageDir{Enter ROMEO}

\tybspeaks Well, peace be with you, sir. Here comes my man.

\mercspeaks But I'll be hanged, sir, if he wear \glos{your livery}{den uniform, som dine tjenere går med, dvs. "gør hvad du siger"}{sb.}:\\
\glos{Marry}{ved jomfru Maria}{udr.}, go before to field, he'll be your follower.\\
Your worship in that sense may call him 'man.'

\tybspeaks Romeo, the hate I bear thee can afford\\
No better term than this: thou art a villain.

\romspeaks Tybalt, the reason that I have to love thee\\
Doth much excuse the appertaining rage\\
To such a greeting. Villain am I none;\\
Therefore farewell; I see thou know'st me not.

\tybspeaks Boy, this shall not excuse the injuries\\
That thou hast done me; therefore turn and draw.

\romspeaks I do protest, I never injured thee,\\
But love thee better than thou canst devise,\\
Till thou shalt know the reason of my love.\\
And so, good Capulet,--which name I tender\\
As dearly as my own,--be satisfied.

\mercspeaks O calm, dishonourable, vile submission!\\
\glos{'Alla stoccata'}{italiensk fægteudtryk}{idiom} carries it away.
\StageDir{Draws}

Tybalt, you rat-catcher, will you walk?

\tybspeaks What wouldst thou have with me?

\mercspeaks Good king of cats, nothing but one of your nine lives; that I mean to make bold withal, and as you shall use me hereafter, drybeat the rest of the eight. Will you pluck your sword out of his \glos{pilcher}{sværdskeden}{sb.} by the ears? Make haste, lest mine be about your ears \glos{ere}{før}{præp.} it be out.

\tybspeaks I am for you.

\StageDir{Drawing}

\romspeaks Gentle Mercutio, put thy rapier up.

\mercspeaks Come, sir, your \glos{passado}{italiensk fægteudtryk}{idiom}.

\StageDir{They fight}

\romspeaks Draw, Benvolio; beat down their weapons.\\
Gentlemen, for shame, forbear this outrage!\\
Tybalt, Mercutio, the prince expressly hath\\
Forbidden bandying in Verona streets:\\
Hold, Tybalt! good Mercutio!

\StageDir{TYBALT under ROMEO's arm stabs MERCUTIO, and flies with his followers}

\mercspeaks \hspace{11em} I am hurt.\\
A plague o' both your houses! I am \glos{sped}{færdig}{adj.}.\\
Is he gone and hath nothing?

\benspeaks \hspace{11em} What, art thou hurt?

\mercspeaks Ay, ay, a scratch, a scratch. Marry, 'tis enough.\\
Where is my page? Go, villain, fetch a surgeon.

\StageDir{Exit Page}

\romspeaks Courage, man; the hurt cannot be much.

\mercspeaks No, 'tis not so deep as a well, nor so wide as a church-door; but 'tis enough,'twill serve: ask for me to-morrow, and you shall find me a grave man. I am \glos{peppered}{færdig}{adj.}, I warrant, for this world. A plague o' both your houses! 'Zounds, a dog, a rat, a mouse, a cat, to scratch a man to death! a braggart, a rogue, a villain, that fights by the book of arithmetic! Why the devil came you between us? I was hurt under your arm.

\romspeaks I thought all for the best.

\mercspeaks Help me into some house, Benvolio,\\
Or I shall faint. A plague o' both your houses!\\
They have made worms' meat of me. I have it,\\
And soundly too. Your houses!

\StageDir{Exeunt MERCUTIO and BENVOLIO}

\romspeaks This gentleman, the prince's near ally,\\
My very friend, hath got his mortal hurt\\
In my behalf; my reputation stain'd\\
With Tybalt's slander,--Tybalt, that an hour\\
Hath been my kinsman! O sweet Juliet,\\
Thy beauty hath made me effeminate\\
And in my temper soften'd valour's steel!

\StageDir{Re-enter BENVOLIO}

\benspeaks O Romeo, Romeo, brave Mercutio's dead!\\
That gallant spirit hath aspired the clouds,\\
Which too untimely here did scorn the earth.

\romspeaks This day's black fate on more days doth depend;\\
This but begins the woe, others must end.

\StageDir{Re-enter TYBALT}

\benspeaks Here comes the furious Tybalt back again.

\romspeaks Alive, in triumph and Mercutio slain?\\
Away to heaven, \glos{respective lenity}{nåde}{idiom},\\
And fire-eyed fury be my conduct now!\\
Now, Tybalt, take the 'villain' back again,\\
That late thou gavest me; for Mercutio's soul\\
Is but a little way above our heads,\\
Staying for thine to keep him company:\\
Either thou, or I, or both, must go with him.

\tybspeaks Thou, wretched boy, that didst consort him here,\\
Shalt with him hence.

\romspeaks \hspace{8em} This shall determine that.

\StageDir{They fight; TYBALT falls and dies}

\benspeaks Romeo, away, be gone!\\
The citizens are up, and Tybalt slain.\\
Stand not amazed: the prince will doom thee death,\\
If thou art taken. Hence, be gone, away!

\romspeaks O, I am fortune's fool!

\benspeaks \hspace{8.5em} Why dost thou stay?

\StageDir{Exit ROMEO}

[...]

\end{drama*}

\benspeaks And what to?

\mercspeaks Nay, \glos{an}{if}{konj.} there were two such, we should have none shortly, for one would kill the other. Thou? Why, thou wilt quarrel with a man that hath a hair more, or a hair less, in his beard, than thou hast. Thou wilt quarrel with a man for cracking nuts, having no other reason but because thou hast hazel eyes. What eye but such an eye would spy out such a quarrel? Thy head is as full of quarrels as an egg is full of \glos{meat}{mad}{sb.}, and yet thy head hath been beaten as addle as an egg for quarrelling. Thou hast quarrelled with a man for coughing in the street, because he hath wakened thy dog that hath lain asleep in the sun. Didst thou not fall out with a tailor for wearing his new doublet before Easter? With another, for tying his new shoes with old riband? And yet thou wilt tutor me from quarrelling!

\benspeaks An I were so apt to quarrel as thou art, any man should buy \glos{the fee-simple}{ejendomsretten}{sb.} of my life for an hour and a quarter.

\mercspeaks The fee-simple? O simple!
